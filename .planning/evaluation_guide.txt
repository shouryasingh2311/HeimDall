1. Problem Statement
Organizations need to monitor distributed infrastructure, applications, networks and security events without
exposing sensitive operational data outside their own environments. Traditional monitoring approaches
create security and operational challenges when raw logs, payloads or detailed telemetry are transferred to a
central platform.
Challenge: Design and develop a secure, AIOps-driven observability platform using a two-tier architecture. A
local collector must collect and process telemetry inside the customer environment, keep sensitive and raw
data local, and send only approved metadata, summaries, health indicators and alerts to a centralized SaaS
platform.
Why this problem matters
   Data exposure risk: shipping raw logs, payloads and credentials to a central platform widens the attack
surface and can breach compliance obligations.
  Operational blind spots: teams running many sites, networks and applications need one consolidated
view without losing local control of their data.
  Alert fatigue: static thresholds create noisy, duplicated alerts; operators need correlated incidents with
likely causes.
  Global operations: customers span regions, time zones and data-residency rules, so the platform must
be designed for that from day one.
What the SaaS platform must deliver
   A consolidated view of infrastructure, network, security and collector health across all monitored
environments.
  AIOps techniques that identify anomalies, correlate related events, highlight likely causes and recommend
operational actions.
  Strict multi-tenant separation so one customer never sees another customer's metadata.
  A longer-term path from assisted operations toward Autonomous Ops, where approved and safe
remediation actions run under appropriate controls.
2. Architecture and Core Requirements
TIER 1: CUSTOMER ENVIRONMENT / LOCAL COLLECTOR
Applications | Servers | Network devices | Security sources
Collect > Filter / Redact > Aggregate > Analyze locally > Apply data policy
Raw logs, payloads, secrets and sensitive details remain local by default. Only approved metadata, summaries,
metrics, health state and alert context leave the environment.
Authenticated and encrypted communication (e.g. TLS), regional ingest endpoint
TIER 2: CENTRAL SAAS PLATFORM (multi-tenant, metadata-only)
Metadata ingestion > Fleet analytics > AIOps correlation > Dashboards / Alerts / Recommendations
Future: policy-controlled, human-approved Autonomous Ops actions.

Local collector and data protection
   Collect infrastructure, application, network and security telemetry from configured local sources (agents,
SNMP, syslog, flow data, APIs).
  Keep raw logs, payloads, secrets and detailed sensitive records inside the customer environment by
default.
  Filter, normalize, redact, aggregate and summarize data locally before transmission.
  Send only allow-listed metadata: metric values, timestamps, pseudonymous source identifiers, health
state, event category, severity and concise alert context.
  Enforce secure collector authentication, encrypted transport, local retention controls and auditable
data-transfer records.
  Provide an egress proof panel: a visible log of exactly what each payload contained before it left the
customer environment.
Observability and AIOps
   Monitor CPU, memory, disk, service availability, bandwidth, latency, packet loss and connectivity.
  Detect repeated authentication failures and suspicious activity using locally processed signals.
  Detect anomalies, correlate related events, prioritize incidents and provide explainable recommendations.
  Show collector online/offline status, last-seen time, version, resource usage and configuration status.
  Support multiple customer environments with tenant-level data separation.
Global-ready design
   Regional ingest: collectors send to the nearest or assigned regional endpoint to reduce latency and
respect jurisdiction.
  Tenant data residency: each tenant's metadata is stored in its chosen region and never replicated
elsewhere without policy approval.
  UTC timestamps: all events carry UTC time plus a source timezone so dashboards render correctly
worldwide.
  Out-of-order handling: late or reordered events are placed by event time, not arrival time, without
corrupting trends.
  Idempotent delivery: every payload has a unique ID so retries after outages never create duplicates.
Real-time performance targets
Measured from timestamps on the organizer-provided simulated telemetry stream. Suggested values

  Security controls: collector authentication, TLS, field allow-listing, secret scrubbing, local retention and

4. Four-State Health Evaluation Inside the Product
The platform should classify collector and monitored-system health using configurable thresholds and clear
reasoning. Teams should explain which signals affect each state and how stale or missing data is handled.

Meaning
Illustrative condition

Healthy

Fair

Elevated latency, resource use, packet loss or recurring warnings.

Collector unavailable, severe service degradation, or a critical

What to check
   Thresholds are configurable and the reason for each state change is shown to the operator.
  Stale or missing data downgrades the state instead of silently showing the last good value.
  State transitions are timestamped in UTC and appear in an event history.

7. Questions for Judges to Ask
Security and data handling
   Exactly what data leaves the local environment, and what stays behind?
  How does the collector decide which fields are allowed to be sent to SaaS?
  How do you prevent secrets or raw log content from being transmitted accidentally?
  How are collectors authenticated and data encrypted?
  Can you open the egress proof panel and show the last payload?
AIOps and operations
   How do you distinguish a real anomaly from a normal usage spike?
  How do you correlate multiple alerts into one incident?
  Can you explain why the platform recommends a particular action?
  What safeguards are needed before automated remediation?
Architecture and reliability
   What happens when SaaS is unreachable?
  How do you avoid losing or duplicating metadata after reconnection?
  How would you scale across many collectors and customer tenants?
  How do you handle regions, tenant data residency, UTC time and out-of-order events?

9. Judge's Guidance
   Do not score a team highly only because its dashboard looks polished. Verify that the collector gathers
data, processes it locally and sends only approved metadata to SaaS. Ask the team to prove what
remains local and what appears centrally.
  Give credit for AIOps that goes beyond static threshold alerts: look for anomaly context, correlation,
prioritization and explainable recommendations.
  Measure the real-time targets from timestamps on the shared telemetry stream, not from team claims.
  Treat Autonomous Ops as a future direction. Safe simulation and human approval are appropriate within a
24-hour hackathon.
Key question: "Can you show exactly what your local collector sends to SaaS, prove that sensitive and raw
data stays within the customer environment, and demonstrate how AIOps turns metadata into an actionable
recommendation?"
Note: The evaluation structure (four judging phases across the hackathon) and the product health classification (four
operational states) are two separate concepts.
